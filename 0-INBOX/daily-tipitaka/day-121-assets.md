## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Satipaṭṭhāna)

[[#^toc-7-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye lokuttarasatipaṭṭhānavasena desanāya āraddhattā yathā kāyādiārammaṇesu lokiyasatipaṭṭhānesu tanti ṭhapitā, na tathā idha; atha kho sabbānipi kāyānupassanādīni satipaṭṭhānāni dhammasaṅgaṇiyaṃ vibhattassa desanānayassa mukhamattameva dassentena niddiṭṭhāni. Nayabhedo panettha evaṃ veditabbo – kāyānupassanāya sotāpattimagge jhānābhinivese suddhikapaṭipadā, suddhikasuññatā, suññatapaṭipadā, suddhikaappaṇihitaṃ, appaṇihitapaṭipadāti imesu pañcasu ṭhānesu dvinnaṃ dvinnaṃ catukkapañcakanayānaṃ vasena dasa nayā; evaṃ vīsatiyā abhinivesesu dve nayasatāni, tāni catūhi adhipatīhi catuguṇitāni aṭṭha satāni, iti suddhikāni dve sādhipatīni aṭṭhāti nayasahassaṃ. Tathā vedanānupassanādīsu suddhikasatipaṭṭhāne cāti sotāpattimagge pañca nayasahassāni; yathā ca sotāpattimagge, evaṃ sesamaggesūti kusale vīsati nayasahassāni, suññatāppaṇihitānimittabhedesu tato tiguṇe vipāke saṭṭhi nayasahassāni. Iti kusale pañcannaṃ vipāke pañcannanti dasannaṃ niddesavārānaṃ vasena dasappabhedo asītinayasahassapatimaṇḍito abhidhammabhājanīyaniddeso.

### Pañhāpucchakaṃ (Satipaṭṭhāna)

[[#^toc-7-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchake pāḷianusāreneva satipaṭṭhānānaṃ kusalādibhāvo veditabbo. Ārammaṇattikesu pana sabbānipi etāni appamāṇaṃ nibbānaṃ ārabbha pavattanato appamāṇārammaṇāneva, na maggārammaṇāni; sahajātahetuvasena pana maggahetukāni; vīriyaṃ vā vīmaṃsaṃ vā jeṭṭhakaṃ katvā maggabhāvanākāle maggādhipatīni; chandacittajeṭṭhakāya pana maggabhāvanāya phalakāle ca navattabbāni; atītādīsu ekārammaṇabhāvenapi navattabbāni; nibbānassa bahiddhādhammattā bahiddhārammaṇāni nāma honti. Evaṃ imasmiṃ pañhāpucchake nibbattitalokuttarāneva satipaṭṭhānāni kathitāni; suttantabhājanīyasmiṃyeva hi sammāsambuddhena lokiyalokuttaramissakā satipaṭṭhānā kathitā, abhidhammabhājanīyapañhāpucchakesu pana lokuttarāyeva. Evamayaṃ satipaṭṭhānavibhaṅgopi teparivaṭṭaṃ nīharitvā bhājetvā dassito.

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Satipaṭṭhāna)

[[#^toc-7-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye lokuttarasatipaṭṭhānavasena desanā āraddhā; tattha kāyādiārammaṇesu tanti aṭṭhapetvā suddhikapaṭipadāsuññatāppaṇihitādibhedena asītinayasahassāni dassitāni. ### Pañhāpucchakaṃ (Satipaṭṭhāna)

[[#^toc-7-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchake nibbattitalokuttarāneva satipaṭṭhānāni kathitāni; tāni appamāṇaṃ nibbānaṃ ārabbha pavattanato appamāṇārammaṇāni, sahajātahetuvasena maggahetukāni, vīriyavīmaṃsajeṭṭhakakāle maggādhipatīni, nibbānassa bahiddhādhammattā bahiddhārammaṇāni. Tasmā yoginā "satipaṭṭhānaṃ maggameva ārammaṇaṃ karotī"ti vā "attano cittameva parittaṃ ārammaṇaṃ hotī"ti vā micchāgāhaṃ pahāya, appamāṇārammaṇataṃ bahiddhārammaṇatañca yathāsabhāvato sallakkhaṇaṃ upasampādetvā, "suttantabhājanīye lokiyalokuttaramissakā, idha pana lokuttarāyevā"ti tiparivaṭṭaṃ desanaṃ anugacchanto, appamāṇe nibbāne ninnapoṇapabbhāracitto hutvā cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

#### The Section of Questions and Answers ^2-7-3-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-386]]

There are four foundations of mindfulness. Here a monk dwells contemplating the body in the body — ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and grief with regard to the world; he dwells contemplating feelings in feelings — ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and grief with regard to the world; he dwells contemplating the mind in the mind — ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and grief with regard to the world; he dwells contemplating mental objects in mental objects — ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and grief with regard to the world. ^2-386

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-387]]

Of the four foundations of mindfulness, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral… how many involve conflict, how many are free from conflict? ^2-387

##### The Triads ^2-7-3-1-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-388]]

They may be wholesome; they may be neutral. They may be associated with pleasant feeling; they may be associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. They may be results; they may be things subject to result. They are not taken up at birth and not able to be clung to. They are undefiled and not connected with defilement. They may be with thought and with examination; they may be without thought but with examination only; they may be without thought and without examination. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be accompanied by happiness; they may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. Their causes are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. They may be leading to dismantling; they may be leading neither to accumulation nor to dismantling. They may belong to one in training; they may belong to one beyond training. They are measureless. They have measureless objects. They are refined. They may be fixed in rightness; they may be unfixed. They do not have the path as their object; they may have the path as their cause; they may have the path as their dominant factor; they may not be describable either as having the path as their cause or as having the path as their dominant factor. They may be arisen; they may be not arisen; they may be due to arise. They may be past; they may be future; they may be present. They are not describable as having a past object, a future object, or a present object. They may be internal; they may be external; they may be both internal and external. They have external objects. They are invisible and offer no resistance. ^2-388

##### The Pairs ^2-7-3-2-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-389]]

They are not roots. They have roots. They are associated with roots. They are not describable as being both roots and having roots; they have roots but are not roots. They are not describable as being both roots and associated with roots; they are associated with roots but are not roots. They are not roots yet have roots.
They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They offer no resistance. They are immaterial. They are beyond the world. They can be known by one means; they cannot be known by another. They are not mental taints. They are without mental taints. They are dissociated from mental taints. They are not describable either as being mental taints and subject to mental taints, or as being subject to mental taints but not mental taints. They are not describable either as being mental taints and associated with mental taints, or as being associated with mental taints but not mental taints. They are dissociated from mental taints. They are without mental taints. They are not fetters… they are not ties… they are not floods… they are not yokes… they are not hindrances… they are not wrong grasping… they have objects. They are not minds. They are mental factors. They are associated with mind. They are conjoined with mind. They are originated by mind. They arise together with mind. They follow along with mind. They are conjoined with and originated by mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and arise together with mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and follow along with mind. They are external. They are not derived. They are not taken up at birth. They are not clinging… they are not defilements… they are not to be abandoned by seeing, not to be abandoned by meditative development. Their causes are not to be abandoned by seeing. Their causes are not to be abandoned by meditative development.
They may be with thought; they may be without thought. They may be with examination; they may be without examination. They may be with joy; they may be without joy. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be not accompanied by joy. They may be accompanied by happiness; they may be not accompanied by happiness. They may be accompanied by equanimity; they may be not accompanied by equanimity. They do not belong to the sense sphere. They do not belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are not included in any sphere. They may be leading out; they may be not leading out. They may be fixed; they may be unfixed. They are unsurpassed. They are free from conflict.
