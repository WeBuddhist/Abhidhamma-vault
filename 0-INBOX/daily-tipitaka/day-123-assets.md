## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Sammappadhāna)

[[#^toc-8-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye sabbānipi sammappadhānāni dhammasaṅgaṇiyaṃ vibhattassa desanānayassa mukhamattameva dassentena niddiṭṭhāni. Nayabhedo panettha – paṭhamasammappadhāne sotāpattimagge jhānābhinivese suddhikapaṭipadā, suddhikasuññatā, suññatapaṭipadā, suddhikaappaṇihitaṃ, appaṇihitapaṭipadāti pañcasu ṭhānesu dvinnaṃ dvinnaṃ catukkapañcakanayānaṃ vasena dasa nayā; evaṃ vīsatiyā abhinivesesu dve nayasatāni, tāni catūhi adhipatīhi catugguṇitāni aṭṭha satāni, iti suddhikāni dve sādhipatīni aṭṭhāti nayasahassaṃ. Tathā dutiyasammappadhānādīsu suddhikasammappadhāne cāti sotāpattimagge pañca nayasahassāni; yathā ca sotāpattimagge evaṃ sesamaggesūti kusalavaseneva vīsati nayasahassāni. **Vipāke pana sammappadhānehi kattabbakiccaṃ natthīti vipākavāro na gahito** – iminā satipaṭṭhānabojjhaṅgavibhaṅgehi ayaṃ viseso veditabbo. Sammappadhānāni cettha nibbattitalokuttarāneva kathitāni.

### Pañhāpucchakaṃ (Sammappadhāna)

[[#^toc-8-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchake pāḷianusāreneva sammappadhānānaṃ kusalādibhāvo veditabbo. Ārammaṇattikesu pana sabbānipi etāni appamāṇaṃ nibbānaṃ ārabbha pavattanato appamāṇārammaṇāneva, na maggārammaṇāni; sahajātahetuvasena pana maggahetukāni; vīmaṃsaṃ jeṭṭhakaṃ katvā maggabhāvanākāle maggādhipatīni; chandacittajeṭṭhikāya maggabhāvanāya na vattabbāni maggādhipatīnīti; **vīriyajeṭṭhikāya pana aññassa vīriyassa abhāvā na vattabbāni maggādhipatīnīti vā na maggādhipatīnīti vā** – sammappadhānaṃ hi sayameva vīriyaṃ, tasmā aññaṃ vīriyaṃ adhipati hutvā na labbhati. Atītādīsu ekārammaṇabhāvenapi na vattabbāni; nibbānassa pana bahiddhādhammattā bahiddhārammaṇāni nāma honti. Evaṃ imasmiṃ pañhāpucchake nibbattitalokuttarāneva sammappadhānāni kathitāni.

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Sammappadhāna)

[[#^toc-8-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīye sammappadhānāni nibbattitalokuttarāneva vīsatinayasahassavasena vibhattāni; **vipāke pana sammappadhānehi kattabbakiccaṃ natthīti vipākavāro na gahito**. Tasmā yoginā "puññaphalena sayameva padhānaṃ nipphajjissatī"ti vipākapaccāsīsanaṃ pahāya, "padhānaṃ nāma kattabbameva, na paṭilabhitabbaṃ; kusalapakkheyeva tassa okāso"ti niṭṭhaṃ upasampādetvā, appamatto hutvā attanā kātabbaṃ kiccaṃ anaññassa nikkhipanto cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

### Pañhāpucchakaṃ (Sammappadhāna)

[[#^toc-8-3|↑↑↑]]

Pañhāpucchake sammappadhānā appamāṇārammaṇā maggahetukā ca vuttā; vīmaṃsajeṭṭhikāya maggabhāvanāya maggādhipatīni, **vīriyajeṭṭhikāya pana aññassa vīriyassa abhāvā na vattabbāneva** – sammappadhānaṃ hi sayameva vīriyaṃ. Tasmā yoginā "aññaṃ kiñci balaṃ nissāya padahissāmī"ti parāyattagāhaṃ pahāya, "yaṃ padahati tadeva vīriyaṃ, natthi tato añño padhānakārako"ti attapaccakkhaṃ dassanaṃ upasampādetvā, appamāṇe nibbāne ārammaṇaṃ ṭhapento cittaṃ pariyodāpetabbaṃ.

Here a monk, on the occasion when he develops the meditative absorption that is beyond the world, leading out, leading to dismantling, for the abandoning of views, for reaching the first stage — secluded from sense pleasures… he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, of difficult practice and slow direct knowledge — the mental initiation of energy… right effort, the awakening factor of energy, a path factor, included in the path, that there is on that occasion: this is called "right effort". The remaining things are associated with right effort.
The Classification According to the Abhidhamma is finished. ^2-426

#### The Section of Questions and Answers ^2-8-3-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-427]]

There are four right efforts. Here a monk generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the non-arising of evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen; for the abandoning of evil, unwholesome things that have arisen… for the arising of wholesome things that have not yet arisen… for the persistence of wholesome things that have arisen, for their non-confusion, their increase, their expansion, their development, their perfection he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours. ^2-427

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-428]]

Of the four right efforts, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral… how many involve conflict, how many are free from conflict? ^2-428

##### The Triads ^2-8-3-1-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-429]]

They are wholesome only. They may be associated with pleasant feeling; they may be associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. They are things subject to result. They are not taken up at birth and not able to be clung to. They are undefiled and not connected with defilement. They may be with thought and with examination; they may be without thought but with examination only; they may be without thought and without examination. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be accompanied by happiness; they may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. Their causes are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. They are leading to dismantling. They belong to one in training. They are measureless. They have measureless objects. They are refined. They are fixed in rightness. They do not have the path as their object. They have the path as their cause. They may have the path as their dominant factor; they may not be describable as having the path as their dominant factor. They may be arisen; they may be not arisen; they are not describable as due to arise. They may be past; they may be future; they may be present. They are not describable as having a past object, a future object, or a present object. They may be internal; they may be external; they may be both internal and external. They have external objects. They are invisible and offer no resistance. ^2-429

##### The Pairs ^2-8-3-2-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-430]]

They are not roots. They have roots. They are associated with roots. They are not describable as being both roots and having roots; they have roots but are not roots. They are not describable as being both roots and associated with roots; they are associated with roots but are not roots. They are not roots yet have roots. They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They offer no resistance. They are immaterial. They are beyond the world. They can be known by one means; they cannot be known by another. They are not mental taints. They are without mental taints. They are dissociated from mental taints. They are not describable either as being mental taints and subject to mental taints, or as being subject to mental taints but not mental taints. They are not describable either as being mental taints and associated with mental taints, or as being associated with mental taints but not mental taints. They are dissociated from mental taints. They are without mental taints. They are not fetters… they are not ties… they are not floods… they are not yokes… they are not hindrances… they are not wrong grasping… they have objects. They are not minds. They are mental factors. They are associated with mind. They are conjoined with mind. They are originated by mind. They arise together with mind. They follow along with mind. They are conjoined with and originated by mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and arise together with mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and follow along with mind. They are external. They are not derived. They are not taken up at birth. They are not clinging… they are not defilements… they are not to be abandoned by seeing. They are not to be abandoned by meditative development. Their causes are not to be abandoned by seeing. Their causes are not to be abandoned by meditative development. They may be with thought; they may be without thought. They may be with examination; they may be without examination. They may be with joy; they may be without joy. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be not accompanied by joy. They may be accompanied by happiness; they may be not accompanied by happiness. They may be accompanied by equanimity; they may be not accompanied by equanimity. They do not belong to the sense sphere. They do not belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are not included in any sphere. They are leading out. They are fixed. They are unsurpassed. They are free from conflict.
The Section of Questions and Answers.
The Analysis of the Right Efforts is finished. ^2-430
