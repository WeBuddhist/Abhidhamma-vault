#### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ ^2-9-1-0

Cattāro iddhipādā – idha bhikkhu chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, vīriyasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, cittasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, vīmaṃsāsamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti. ^2-431

##### Chandiddhipādo ^2-9-1-1-0

Kathañca bhikkhu chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti? Chandaṃ ce bhikkhu adhipatiṃ karitvā labhati samādhiṃ, labhati cittassekaggataṃ [cittassa ekaggataṃ (sī. syā.)] – ayaṃ vuccati "chandasamādhi". So anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, uppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, anuppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ uppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Ime vuccanti "padhānasaṅkhārā". Iti ayañca chandasamādhi, ime ca padhānasaṅkhārā. Tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññahitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārotveva saṅkhyaṃ [saṅkhaṃ (sī.)] gacchati. ^2-432

Tattha katamo chando? Yo chando chandikatā kattukamyatā kusalo dhammacchando – ayaṃ vuccati "chando".
Tattha katamo samādhi? Yā cittassa ṭhiti saṇṭhiti avaṭṭhiti avisāhāro avikkhepo avisāhaṭamānasatā samatho samādhindriyaṃ samādhibalaṃ sammāsamādhi – ayaṃ vuccati "samādhi".
Tattha katamo padhānasaṅkhāro? Yo cetasiko vīriyārambho nikkamo parakkamo uyyāmo vāyāmo ussāho ussoḷhī thāmo ṭhiti asithilaparakkamatā anikkhittachandatā anikkhittadhuratā dhurasampaggāho vīriyaṃ vīriyindriyaṃ vīriyabalaṃ sammāvāyāmo – ayaṃ vuccati "padhānasaṅkhāro". Iti iminā ca chandena, iminā ca samādhinā, iminā ca padhānasaṅkhārena upeto hoti samupeto upāgato samupāgato upapanno sampanno samannāgato. Tena vuccati "chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgato"ti. ^2-433

Iddhīti. Yā tesaṃ dhammānaṃ iddhi samiddhi ijjhanā samijjhanā lābho paṭilābho patti sampatti phusanā sacchikiriyā upasampadā.
"Iddhipādo"ti. Tathābhūtassa vedanākkhandho, saññākkhandho, saṅkhārakkhandho, viññāṇakkhandho.
"Iddhipādaṃ bhāvetī"ti. Te dhamme āsevati bhāveti bahulīkaroti. Tena vuccati "iddhipādaṃ bhāvetī"ti. ^2-434

## Section summary (from `2-RAILS/Sections/pi-2-summaries.md`)

### Suttantabhājanīyaṃ (Iddhipāda)

[[#^toc-9-1|↑↑↑]]

Chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgatanti ettha chandahetuko chandādhiko vā samādhi chandasamādhi – kattukamyatāchandaṃ adhipatiṃ karitvā paṭiladdhasamādhissetaṃ adhivacanaṃ; padhānabhūtā saṅkhārā padhānasaṅkhārā – catukiccasādhakassa sammappadhānavīriyassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ; iddhipādoti tesaṃ adhiṭṭhānaṭṭhena pādabhūto sesacittacetasikarāsi. Yatheva chandaṃ adhipatiṃ karitvā paṭiladdhasamādhi chandasamādhīti vutto, evaṃ vīriyaṃ cittaṃ vīmaṃsañca adhipatiṃ karitvā paṭiladdhā vīriyacittavīmaṃsasamādhīti vuccanti; **sesaiddhipādaniddesesupi imināva nayena attho veditabbo**, tasmā ekāva ayaṃ vaṇṇanā catunnampi iddhipādānaṃ sādhāraṇā. Ettāvatā catunnaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ matthakappattaṃ kammaṭṭhānaṃ kathitaṃ – eko hi chandaṃ avassayati, eko vīriyaṃ, eko cittaṃ, eko paññaṃ; yathā ṭhānantaraṃ patthentesu catūsu amaccaputtesu eko upaṭṭhānaṃ eko sūrabhāvaṃ eko jātiṃ eko mantaṃ avassāya sabbepi ṭhānantarāni pāpuṇiṃsu, evaṃ chandadhurena raṭṭhapālatthero, vīriyadhurena soṇatthero, cittadhurena sambhūtatthero, vīmaṃsādhurena mogharājatthero lokuttaradhammaṃ nibbattesuṃ. Abhedato tayo chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasaṅkhātā dhammā iddhīpi honti iddhipādāpi, sesā sampayuttakā cattāro khandhā iddhipādāyeva; bhedato pana chando iddhi, chandadhurena bhāvitā cattāro khandhā chandiddhipādo. Pubbabhāgo ca iddhipādo nāma, paṭilābho iddhi nāma – paṭhamajjhānaparikammaṃ iddhipādo, paṭhamajjhānaṃ iddhi; sotāpattimaggassa vipassanā iddhipādo, sotāpattimaggo iddhi. Iddhipi iddhipādopi nipphannā tilakkhaṇabbhāhatāti sanniṭṭhānaṃ kataṃ.

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Iddhipāda)

[[#^toc-9-2|↑↑↑]]

Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ uttānatthameva; nayā panettha gaṇetabbā. "Chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāvetī"ti vuttaṭṭhāne lokuttarāni cattāri nayasahassāni vibhattāni; vīriyasamādhiādīsupi eseva nayo. Tathā uttaracūḷabhājanīye chandiddhipāde cattāri nayasahassāni, vīriyacittavīmaṃsiddhipādesu cattāri cattārīti sabbānipi aṭṭhannaṃ catukkānaṃ vasena dvattiṃsa nayasahassāni vibhattāni. Uttaracūḷabhājanīye ca "chandoyeva chandiddhipādo, vīriyameva, cittameva, vīmaṃsāva vīmaṃsiddhipādo"ti kathitaṃ; tena "iddhipādo sampayuttakānaṃ catunnaṃ khandhānaṃyeva adhivacana"nti ekaṃsagāho na gahetabbo. Evametaṃ nibbattitalokuttarānaṃyeva iddhipādānaṃ vasena dvattiṃsanayasahassappaṭimaṇḍitaṃ abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ kathitaṃ.

### Abhidhammabhājanīyaṃ (Iddhipāda)

Abhidhammabhājanīye uttaracūḷabhājanīyavasena "chandoyeva chandiddhipādo, vīriyameva, cittameva, vīmaṃsāva vīmaṃsiddhipādo"ti kathitaṃ, dvattiṃsa ca nayasahassāni nibbattitalokuttarānaṃyeva vasena vibhattāni. ## English translation (from `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-Contemporary-English-Abhidhamma/pi-2-english-plain-zeroshot.md`)

There are four bases of success. Here a monk develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through desire and the formations of endeavour; he develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through energy and the formations of endeavour; he develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through mind and the formations of endeavour; he develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through investigation and the formations of endeavour. ^2-431

##### The Basis of Success Through Desire ^2-9-1-1-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-432]]

And how does a monk develop the basis of success endowed with concentration through desire and the formations of endeavour? If a monk, making desire the dominant factor, obtains concentration, obtains one-pointedness of mind — this is called "concentration through desire". He generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the non-arising of evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen; he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the abandoning of evil, unwholesome things that have arisen; he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the arising of wholesome things that have not yet arisen; he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the persistence of wholesome things that have arisen, for their non-confusion, their increase, their expansion, their development, their perfection. These are called "the formations of endeavour". So there is this concentration through desire and there are these formations of endeavour. Gathering them together and summing them up, they are reckoned simply as "concentration through desire and the formations of endeavour". ^2-432

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-433]]

Therein, what is desire? The desire, wishing, wish to act, wholesome desire for the Dhamma — this is called "desire".
Therein, what is concentration? The steadiness of the mind, its stability, its firm standing, its non-wavering, non-distraction, the state of an undistracted mind, calm, the faculty of concentration, the power of concentration, right concentration — this is called "concentration".
Therein, what is the formation of endeavour? The mental initiation of energy, exertion, striving forward, effort, striving, zeal, vigour, firmness, steadiness, unrelaxed striving, undiminished desire, undiminished responsibility, taking up the burden, energy, the faculty of energy, the power of energy, right effort — this is called "the formation of endeavour". So he is endowed with this desire, this concentration, and this formation of endeavour, fully endowed, having reached them, fully reached them, possessed of them, complete in them, furnished with them. Therefore it is said, "endowed with concentration through desire and the formations of endeavour". ^2-433

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-434]]

"Success": the success of those things, their prospering, succeeding, fully succeeding, gain, obtaining, attainment, full attainment, touching, realisation, achievement.
"Basis of success": for one who is such, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of mental formations, the aggregate of consciousness.
"Develops the basis of success": he practises those things, develops them, cultivates them. Therefore it is said, "develops the basis of success". ^2-434

##### The Basis of Success Through Desire ^2-9-2-1-0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-445]]

And how does a monk develop the basis of success endowed with concentration through desire and the formations of endeavour? Here a monk, on the occasion when he develops the meditative absorption that is beyond the world, leading out, leading to dismantling, for the abandoning of views, for reaching the first stage — secluded from sense pleasures… he enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, of difficult practice and slow direct knowledge — on that occasion he develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through desire and the formations of endeavour. ^2-445

![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-2.md#^2-446]]

Therein, what is desire? The desire, wishing, wish to act, wholesome desire for the Dhamma — this is called "desire".
Therein, what is concentration? The steadiness of the mind, its stability… right concentration, the awakening factor of concentration, a path factor, included in the path — this is called "concentration".
Therein, what is the formation of endeavour? The mental initiation of energy… right effort, the awakening factor of energy, a path factor, included in the path — this is called "the formation of endeavour". So he is endowed with this desire, this concentration, and this formation of endeavour… furnished with them. Therefore it is said, "endowed with concentration through desire and the formations of endeavour". ^2-446
