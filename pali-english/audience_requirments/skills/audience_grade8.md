# Audience Profile: Grade 8 Students

## Audience

Students approximately **13–14 years old** (around **grade 8** in their own school system).

**English is their second or third language** — not their main language at home. They read English for school, but they are **not** native English readers.

**Assume they:**

* can follow clear school English on familiar topics
* understand some abstract words (emotion, reason, habit) when spelled plainly
* have **little or no** formal Buddhist training

**Do not assume they know:** native-level fluency, idioms, Abhidhamma categories, Pāli technical vocabulary, or Buddhist Studies conventions.

---

## Translation Goal

Communicate the meaning **clearly** in plain school English — like a clear textbook for language learners, not native literary prose and not a scholarly translation.

---

## Terminology

Use plain English a teenage **L2/L3 learner** can follow. For each doctrinal sense, pick **one** rendering and reuse it **every time** that sense appears.

### Word-choice rules (grade 8)

| Rule | Do | Don't |
| ---- | -- | ----- |
| Length | One clear phrase; a brief qualifier in parentheses is OK | Multi-clause scholarly definitions |
| English level | Common school vocabulary; explain in the phrase if needed | Idioms, slang, rare words, nested clauses |
| Register | Plain modern English | Hedonic tone, karmically profitable, supra-mundane |
| Abhidhamma | Short explanations ("the five groups that make up experience", "mental habit") | Untranslated jargon (khandha, āyatana, cetasika) |
| Pāli loans | **Buddha**, **Dhamma**, **Nibbāna** when established in context; otherwise explain | Stacking many loanwords in one sentence |
| Synonym pick | Simplest cluster synonym that preserves the **distinction** between senses | Collapsing different Sense Tags into one English word |

### Examples (lemma, sense → rendering)

* citta, `mind_heart` → mind
* vedanā, `pleasant_unpleasant` → feeling — pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral
* saṅkhāra, `mental_formation` → mental habits and intentions
* dhamma, `teaching_discourse` → the Buddha's teaching
* dhamma, `matter_thing` → something that comes into being and passes away
* kilesa, `defilement_impurity_unwholesome` → an unwholesome mental state

For other doctrinal terms, choose one accessible phrase and reuse it for that sense everywhere.

Avoid specialized philosophical vocabulary unless the source requires it and you can state it plainly.

---

## Style

* **Direct** and **readable**
* **Short to medium** sentences — prefer two short sentences over one long one
* Plain modern English (not archaic, idiomatic, or overly formal)
* **Consistent** word choices — L2/L3 readers learn terms from repetition

---

## Explanatory Additions

Small clarifications **through wording** are permitted (e.g. "mental state" instead of a single opaque word).

Do not add new concepts or commentary absent from the source.

---

## Priority

1. **Understanding**
2. **Clarity** for an English language learner at this age
3. **Accuracy**
4. **Readability**
