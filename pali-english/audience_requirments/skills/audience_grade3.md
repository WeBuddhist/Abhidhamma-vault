# Audience Profile: Grade 3 Students

## Audience

Children approximately **8–9 years old** (around **grade 3** in their own school system).

**English is their second or third language** — not their main language at home. They may study in English at school, but they are **not** native English readers.

**Assume they:**

* know basic English words from school (mind, feel, good, bad, body, help, hurt)
* are still building reading fluency in English
* have **no** Buddhist or Abhidhamma background

**Do not assume they know:** native-level English, idioms, rare words, meditation jargon, philosophy terms, or Pāli/Sanskrit vocabulary.

---

## Translation Goal

Tell the **main idea** of each passage in **very simple English** a child can follow without a dictionary.

Write for a **young ESL/EFL learner** — clear classroom English, not literary prose and not adult Dhamma talk.

---

## Terminology

Choose words a child can understand. For each doctrinal sense, pick **one** short rendering and reuse it **every time** that sense appears in the text.

### Word-choice rules (grade 3)

| Rule | Do | Don't |
| ---- | -- | ----- |
| Length | **1–4 words** when possible; one short phrase if needed | Long clauses or stacked abstractions |
| English level | **High-frequency** words (feel, think, good, bad, calm, help) | Idioms, phrasal verbs, rare or literary words |
| Register | Simple words a child learns early in English class | Scholarly paraphrase ("arises and passes away", "volitional formations") |
| Abhidhamma | Replace with **body, mind, feeling, habit, good, bad, calm, path** | aggregate, phenomenon, faculty, indeterminate, supra-mundane |
| Pāli loans | Avoid except **Buddha** (and **Dhamma** only if the passage already uses it and context makes it clear) | Nibbāna, saṅkhāra, khandha, cetasika, etc. |
| Synonym pick | Choose the **simplest word in the glossary Sense cluster** that still fits | Copy the Sense column or pick the most doctrinally precise synonym |
| Explain in the word | Light, concrete hint is OK ("good for the mind", "how something feels") | Doctrinal definitions disguised as glosses |

### Examples (lemma, sense → rendering)

* citta, `mind_heart` → mind
* vedanā, `pleasant_unpleasant` → how something feels — good, bad, or neutral
* saṅkhāra, `mental_formation` → habits and intentions in the mind
* dhamma, `teaching_discourse` → the Buddha's teaching
* dhamma, `matter_thing` → something that comes and goes
* kilesa, `defilement_impurity_unwholesome` → a harmful habit of mind

For other doctrinal terms (e.g. saññā → recognizing things), invent **one** short child-friendly phrase and reuse it for that sense everywhere.

---

## Style

* **Very simple** sentences — mostly **one idea**, **one clause**
* **Concrete** — things you can picture (body, food, path, pain, joy)
* **Active** verbs (sees, feels, tries, stops)
* **Short** — if a sentence needs rereading, shorten it
* **No idioms** — say "lets go of" not "gives up the ghost"; say "is angry" not "flies off the handle"
* **Repeat** key words for clarity — L2/L3 readers rely on consistency

---

## Explanatory Additions

Light simplification is allowed (e.g. "the Buddha" instead of "the Blessed One").

Do **not** add facts or stories that are not in the source. Do **not** turn the translation into a lesson.

---

## Priority

1. A child can **understand** it on first read **without a dictionary**
2. **Simple**, high-frequency English words
3. **Core meaning** of the passage
4. **Accuracy** — do not change what the text is saying
