# Day 119 — Where Mindfulness Stands

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

A new chapter opens today. The last one traced how one thing conditions the next. This one asks a simpler question: while all that is happening, where do you put your attention?

Verse 355 names four places — the body, feelings, the mind, and what passes through the mind. Each is watched three ways: in yourself, in another, in both. And each is watched in the same manner — *ātāpī* (ardent), clearly comprehending, mindful, with covetousness and displeasure set down. Then the text slows and takes them one at a time. The body first: hair, nails, teeth, skin and the rest, from the soles up and from the scalp down. Then feelings — pleasant, painful, neither. Then, at verse 365, the mind itself. Is there greed here, or not? Hate, or not? Is it contracted, or scattered?

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Can I name what is in my mind right now, without arguing with it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SATIPAṬṬHĀNA — Foundation of Mindfulness

**Pronunciation:** *sa-ti-paṭ-ṭhā-na* — five syllables. Say "sa-ti-PAT-thaa-na"; the "ṭṭh" is a hard "t" held a beat longer.

**In today's passage:** A *satipaṭṭhāna* is a place where mindfulness is set up and stays. The word joins *sati* (mindfulness — holding something in view) with *paṭṭhāna*, a standing-place. Today's verses name four: body, feelings, mind, and what the mind meets. The teaching says four, no more and no fewer, because there are four ways we get it wrong — taking the body as lovely, feeling as pleasure, the mind as lasting, and the whole of it as a self.

**Why it matters:** You need no new object to practise on. You already have a body, feelings, and a mind. The four foundations say: stand there. Whatever is happening is enough to watch, and the watching is what loosens those four wrong takes.
