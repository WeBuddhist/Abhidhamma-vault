# Day 112 — Again, For Every Mind

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The four angles are done. Today closes the last of them, then does something that shapes the rest of the chapter: it starts over.

Verse 280 asks the opening question again — *which things are unwholesome?* — but for a different mind. Joy with a wrong view, this time with prompting. Then joy without the view. Then the minds rooted in aversion, in doubt, in restlessness. Each one gets the whole treatment: nine starting points, four angles, the full run. And where a link is missing, another factor stands in its place. In a mind with no craving in it, aversion fills the slot. In a doubting mind, doubt does. The pattern does not break. It adapts.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Not only my worst mind — every mind I have?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### AKUSALA — Unwholesome

**Pronunciation:** *a-ku-sa-la* — four syllables. Say "a-KU-sa-la."

**In today's passage:** *Akusala* means unwholesome — what does harm, and what grows from greed, hate, or delusion. Today the analysis stops treating one unwholesome mind as its example and turns to all of them, one after another.

**Why it matters:** It would be easier if only the dramatic states worked this way. The point of starting over is that they all do. The mild irritation, the small doubt, the restless scroll — each is a full turn of the same chain. That is sobering. It is also useful: the same chain anywhere means the same handle anywhere.
