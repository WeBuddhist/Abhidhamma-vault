# Day 117 — Just Doing

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

The indeterminate section closes today. First the last results of unwholesome action. Then something different: the *kiriya* minds — functional, mere doing. Neither wholesome nor unwholesome, and not a result of anything either. They simply work. The mind element that turns toward an object, the mind-consciousness that settles it, and the *jhāna* minds of one for whom nothing further needs making.

Then a new section opens at verse 334, and it is a surprise. *Which things are wholesome?* — again. But this time the chain begins with ignorance conditioning the wholesome. How can that be? Not by sitting in the same moment. By being the reason the practice is happening at all. Someone who has not yet ended ignorance is the only kind of person who develops the path that ends it.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Is my not-knowing a reason to stop — or the reason to start?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### KIRIYA — Functional

**Pronunciation:** *ki-ri-ya* — three syllables. Say "KI-ri-ya."

**In today's passage:** *Kiriya* means functional — mere doing. These minds are not wholesome, not unwholesome, and not results either. They do a job and pass: turning toward an object, settling it, and, in one whose work is finished, the meditation itself.

**Why it matters:** Not everything the mind does is being scored. Some of it is just the machinery working. Seeing that is a relief. It also sharpens the eye for the moments that *are* being scored, which are fewer than they feel.
