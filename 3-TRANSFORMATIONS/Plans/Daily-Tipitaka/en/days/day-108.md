# Day 108 — One Moment, Four Passes

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Now the first angle is spelled out. One unwholesome moment of mind — a mind with joy in it, holding a wrong view — is walked through link by link. Then again. Then twice more. Four passes over the same moment.

Why four? The teachers give reasons. The first pass covers every case. The second names a special condition: contact resting on mentality alone, the eleven-link form the Buddha used in the Great Discourse on Causation. The third fits beings born from a womb, the fourth those who appear all at once. Read another way, the first two show the formless realm, the third the fine-material, the fourth the sense sphere we live in. And notice one small word. Not *formations*, plural, as in the suttas — *formation*, singular. In one moment of mind there is only one intention.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *One moment. How much is going on inside it?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### CITTAKKHAṆA — Mind-Moment

**Pronunciation:** *cit-tak-kha-ṇa* — four beats. Say "chit-tak-KHA-na."

**In today's passage:** A *cittakkhaṇa* is a mind-moment — one arising of consciousness, and gone. The suttas usually spread the twelve links across lifetimes. Here the whole chain is shown inside one of these moments. That is the Abhidhamma method's whole claim: it is not only long ago, it is now.

**Why it matters:** If the round takes lifetimes, there is nothing to do about it today. If it fits inside a moment, there is. The moment you just had was built out of these links. So is the next one.
