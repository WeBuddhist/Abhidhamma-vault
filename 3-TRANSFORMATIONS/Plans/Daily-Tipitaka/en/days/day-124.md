# Day 124 — Four Ways In

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

A new chapter, and a generous one. Four *iddhipāda* — means to accomplishment. Each has the same shape: a concentration reached by putting one thing out in front, together with the exertion that carries it. What changes is which thing goes in front. Aspiration for one person. Energy for another. Mind for a third. Investigation for a fourth.

The teaching reads these as four temperaments rather than four techniques. It tells of four courtiers after the same post — one leaned on service, one on courage, one on birth, one on good counsel — and all four arrived. Then it names four elders who reached the same goal each by a different lead: Raṭṭhapāla by aspiration, Soṇa by energy, Sambhūta by mind, Mogharāja by investigation.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Which of the four actually gets me to sit down — and am I still borrowing someone else's?*

**v. 446.** Therein, what is aspiration? The aspiration, wishing, wish to act, wholesome aspiration for the Dhamma — this is called "aspiration". *(…)* Therein, what is the formation of endeavour? The mental initiation of energy… right effort, the awakening factor of energy, a path factor, included in the path — this is called "the formation of endeavour". So he is endowed with this aspiration, this concentration, and this formation of endeavour… furnished with them. Therefore it is said, "endowed with concentration through aspiration and the formations of endeavour".

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### IDDHIPĀDA — Means to Accomplishment

**Pronunciation:** *id-dhi-pā-da* — four syllables. Say "ID-dhi-paa-da".

**In today's passage:** *Iddhi* is a thing coming off, succeeding. *Pāda* is a foot — a place to stand, and so a way to get somewhere. The compound can be read both ways and the teaching keeps both: the accomplishment itself, and what you stand on to reach it. Today's verses name four such footings.

**Why it matters:** The word is often heard as something exotic. It is not. Whatever stage you are at is the footing for the next one — this morning's sitting is the footing for what comes of it. You do not need a different foot. You need to notice which one is already holding your weight.
