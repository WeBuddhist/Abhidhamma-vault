# Day 115 — Neither Good Nor Bad

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

A third section opens: the indeterminate — states that are neither wholesome nor unwholesome. Today they are results. Because sense-sphere wholesome action was done and stored up, eye-consciousness arises. Then ear-consciousness. Then the mind element. Then mind-consciousness. Bare seeing, bare hearing, bare receiving.

The teachers point out what falls away here. In these minds there is no ignorance at the head of the chain, and nothing to put in that place either — a bare moment of seeing has no root at all. Craving's place is empty too, and clinging's, for the same reason: nothing in a moment of bare seeing is strong enough to fill them. So the chain runs shorter. Not every mind carries the whole apparatus.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *Before I liked or disliked it — was there just seeing?*

**v. 306.** Which things are indeterminate? On the occasion when, because sense-sphere wholesome action has been done and accumulated, resultant eye-consciousness has arisen, accompanied by equanimity, with a sight as its object — on that occasion, with the formation as condition, consciousness; with consciousness as condition, mentality; with mentality as condition, the sixth sense-field; with the sixth sense-field as condition, contact; with contact as condition, feeling; with feeling as condition, existence; with existence as condition, birth; with birth as condition, aging and death. **v. 316.** Therein, what is the formation? The intention, intending, state of intending — this is called "the formation". *(…)* Therein, what is the existence that has decision as condition? Setting aside decision: the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of mental formations, the aggregate of consciousness — this is called "the existence that has decision as condition"… therefore it is said, "thus there is the origin of this entire mass of suffering".

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### ABYĀKATA — Indeterminate

**Pronunciation:** *a-byā-ka-ta* — four syllables. Say "a-BYAA-ka-ta."

**In today's passage:** *Abyākata* means indeterminate — neither wholesome nor unwholesome. Today's minds are results of past action: bare seeing, bare hearing, bare receiving. They carry no blame and earn no credit. They are simply what arrived.

**Why it matters:** A great deal of the mind is like this. The seeing is not the problem; what we do next is. Knowing the difference lets you stop arguing with what has already landed, and put your care where it can still do something.
