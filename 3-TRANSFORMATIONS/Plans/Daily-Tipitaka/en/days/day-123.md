# Day 123 — Nothing Arrives By Itself

## 1. 🧭 Today's Chanting Guide

Today finishes chapter 8. The Abhidhamma section keeps defining the terms of the formula — exerting the mind, endeavouring — and then at verse 427 the question section asks the four right efforts the standard questions: wholesome or not, which feeling goes with them, what they take as their object.

One answer is worth stopping on, and it is an answer by absence. Other chapters get a whole round of questions about results — what arises when past action ripens. This chapter does not. The teaching gives the reason in a line: there is no work for a right effort to do on the result side. A result is received. An effort is made. You can inherit the fruit of yesterday's effort; nobody has ever inherited an effort.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What am I waiting to be given, that I could only ever do?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PADHĀNA — Exertion

**Pronunciation:** *pa-dhā-na* — three syllables. Say "pa-DHAA-na".

**In today's passage:** *Padhāna* is the plain word for putting effort in. It is the last of the five verbs in the formula, after aspiration, striving, energy and exerting the mind, and verse 390 defines it as the energy that does the work of all four right efforts at once.

**Why it matters:** The teaching keeps *padhāna* out of the results section altogether. That is a small structural fact with a large edge on it: nothing on this list happens to you. Whatever gets exerted today, you are the one who exerts it — and that is good news, because it is the part you hold.
